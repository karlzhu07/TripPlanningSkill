# Trip Planning EN

Use this skill when the user wants to design, refine, research, budget, or document an international or domestic trip.

## Workflow

1. Interview the traveler in rounds before drafting a complete itinerary. Ask the current decision frontier only: dates and flexibility, origin, travelers, budget, purpose, pace, accommodation, transport, food, health, safety, and booking constraints.
2. Number each question, explain why it matters, give realistic choices, and recommend a choice with its trade-off. Detect contradictions such as too many activities for a supposedly relaxing trip, mismatched nights and dates, or an unsafe same-day transfer before proceeding.
3. After each round, wait for the answers. Do not silently assume unresolved decisions. Stop interviewing only after the traveler confirms the shared framework.
4. Browse for volatile facts: flight schedules and fares, visa rules, hotel availability and prices, opening hours, transport conditions, local holidays, safety guidance, SIM/eSIM products, and restaurant reviews. Prefer official government, airline, hotel, telecom, tourism-board, and Michelin sources.
5. Never invent flight numbers, prices, review scores, opening hours, visa rules, or hotel facilities. Label estimates, research dates, source quality, and facts that must be checked again before booking.

## Planning principles

- Optimize for safety, comfort, good sleep, meaningful local experiences, and deliberate free time, not maximum attraction count.
- Treat distinctive hotels as destinations. Keep at least part of each day flexible and limit early-morning activities.
- Prefer reputable taxis, hotel transfers, business cars, or private transfers for critical journeys. Do not default to motorbike rental.
- Keep a buffer before international flights. If a route involves boats, remote resorts, border crossings, or uncertain roads, avoid a risky same-day connection to a flight.
- Use a tiered plan: must-do, recommended, optional, and not recommended. Include rain, delay, fatigue, closure, and cancellation alternatives.

## Restaurant research

For every recommendation, provide the name, location, cuisine, rating, review count, review platform, date checked, fit for the itinerary, price level when available, reservation advice, and important recent negative-review themes. Do not treat a high rating with a tiny sample size as strong evidence. Recheck opening hours and holiday operations before booking.

## Deliverable

After confirmation, produce a practical plan with destination order and rationale, detailed time-blocked daily schedules, food and coffee options, transport, accommodation, visa and connectivity steps, booking deadlines, detailed budget, safety and health notes, contingency plans, source links, and the three actions to take now.

If a PDF is requested, create a polished printable document with readable tables, source notes, dynamic-information dates, and a disclaimer. Render and inspect every page for missing glyphs, clipping, overlap, broken tables, and page-number problems before delivery.
